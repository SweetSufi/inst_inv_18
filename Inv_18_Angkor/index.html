A gallery in the reign of Suryavarman II

Angkor Wat was commissioned under King Suryavarman II, whose reign is generally dated 1113 to about 1150. This imagined view places a substantially completed gallery around 1150, when the monument was relatively new. The long sandstone colonnade, relief decoration and corbelled stone construction evoke its architectural language; the particular combination of carvings and distant structures is an artistic synthesis, not a measured reconstruction of a specific corridor.

Behind the Investigation: Research Note

Behind the Investigation explains the generative AI capabilities, technologies, and design decisions demonstrated by each Institute investigation.

Investigation
18 — Meanwhile, in 1967... / Angkor Wat, 1150 CE
Primary Capability
Historical synthesis and visual storytelling: combining a documented architectural tradition with a carefully qualified AI-assisted reconstruction.
Research Question
How can an immersive visual scene convey a monument near its construction era without confusing reconstructed appearance with archaeological certainty?
Method
Use a contemporary gallery photograph for perspective only, apply historical date constraints, generate new environmental artwork, then place interactive object annotations over the image.
How It Works
Six discreet circular hotspots reveal prewritten information about gallery features and historical context. The interface is not embedded in the artwork.
Technical Implementation
Static HTML, CSS and deterministic JavaScript. AI was used to assist image creation and website development; no live language model is called when visitors select a hotspot.
Evidence and Limitations
Angkor Wat's association with Suryavarman II and its sandstone galleries, corbelled construction and sculpted decoration are established. The exact completion date of individual galleries, the decorative motifs shown here, original surface finishes, and the view beyond the colonnade cannot be confirmed from this image. This is an interpretive reconstruction, not a documentary photograph of 1150.
What This Demonstrates
Object-based historical exploration with explicit separation of architectural evidence and visual inference.
